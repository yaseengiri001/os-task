Summary
  The log answers who did what, to which object, when, and whether
  it succeeded. Chaining each entry to its predecessor with a keyed
  HMAC means modification, deletion, insertion and reordering are
  all detectable, and the failure is localised to an entry.
  Secrets are never logged, since logs travel more widely than the
  data they describe.
  The honest limit: the key is on this machine, so an attacker with
  root could forge a consistent log. Real deployments ship entries
  off the host as they are written, which is what puts them beyond
  a local attacker's reach.
